Project of the week / November 16, 2026

When does a busy scene need an alert?

I built a queue monitor and a parking-occupancy demo to explore that question. Two fine-tuned YOLO models turn recorded camera frames into counts, time in a zone and a signal that stays above a threshold.

Built November 15–16, 2026 · Python, YOLO11, ByteTrack and OpenCV

01 / People in a line

A detection becomes a queue signal.

The people model was fine-tuned on a small CrowdHuman subset. ByteTrack keeps an ID for each person, and a polygon marks the queue area. Someone must stay inside for three seconds before they count. A rolling median smooths the count; six people for four seconds triggers the demo alert.

Time in the zone starts when a track first appears there. It is a dwell measurement, not a verified customer wait. Track loss can reset it. The saved model-and-tracker timing was 168.9 ms per frame on my laptop CPU; the video plays at the source frame rate.

02 / Empty or occupied?

Count spaces, then watch the lot fill.

A stock car detector cannot identify an empty parking space. I fine-tuned YOLO11n-OBB on PKLot with two classes: empty and occupied. Rotated boxes fit the angled stalls. The panel shows the prediction beside the dataset labels, and an alert appears after three snapshots at 85% occupancy or higher.

The number of spaces comes from the annotated camera layout. Counts are direct detections; overlapping empty and occupied boxes can occur on one stall. Snapshots are roughly five minutes apart, and playback is accelerated. The alert’s 15-minute label assumes that interval.

04 / The recording I would keep in the review

The afternoon sun breaks the parking count.

On PUCPR, bright pavement and a hard shadow line cause occupied spaces to be read as empty. The overall detection score hides how bad the count becomes during part of the day. Keeping the model and label curves on screen makes that failure visible.

A later short brightness-augmentation experiment did not establish a reliable fix. Better lighting coverage and a separate evaluation of fixed-stall matching are next steps. The head-detection variant also produced unstable queue tracking in its original configuration, so the people-based recording is the main demo.
